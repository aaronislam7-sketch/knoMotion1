# The FIFA World Cup: How It Works and Why It Matters

## What it is
The FIFA World Cup is the world's most-watched sporting event — an international
football (soccer) tournament for senior men's national teams, organised by FIFA,
the sport's global governing body. It is held once every four years. A separate
FIFA Women's World Cup follows the same four-year cycle.

## A brief history
The first World Cup was held in 1930 in Uruguay, with only 13 invited teams.
The host nation, Uruguay, won that inaugural tournament. The competition has run
every four years since, pausing only for the Second World War (no tournaments in
1942 or 1946).

## How a team reaches the World Cup
Reaching the World Cup happens in two stages. First, qualification: national teams
compete within their continental confederation (such as UEFA in Europe or CONMEBOL
in South America) for a limited number of places. Second, the finals: the qualified
teams travel to the host country for the month-long final tournament.
A common misconception is that every country plays in the finals — in fact only a
few dozen teams qualify out of more than 200 national associations.

## The format of the finals
Historically the finals featured 32 teams, drawn into groups for a group stage
(three points for a win, one for a draw). The top teams advance to a single-
elimination knockout stage, culminating in the final. From the 2026 tournament —
co-hosted by the United States, Canada and Mexico — the finals expand to 48 teams.

## Who wins
Brazil is the most successful nation with five titles; Germany and Italy have also
won multiple times. Champions receive the trophy but return it before the next
edition and keep a replica — another misconception is that the winner keeps the
original trophy permanently.

## Why it matters
Beyond sport, the World Cup is a cultural and economic phenomenon: billions of
cumulative viewers, national pride, tourism and investment for hosts, and players
who become global icons.
